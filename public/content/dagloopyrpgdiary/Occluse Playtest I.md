# Occluse Playtest I

After many years of ideating and about 6 drafts of the RPG I wanted to make, I finally started playtesting it.

The playtest game is set in Solemnity, the city of jewels, 80 years after the events of The Ruby Crown campaign (documented on this blog).

The rules text we used is [Occluse Again - Core Playtest 30 Aug](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1kA_DdviFz2f9hMhNd02ppH9esjNJ5Jt5vGjSSX9AOoY/edit?tab=t.0#heading=h.kph6utvtvmd2) and it's regional companion [Occluse Again - City of Jewels Playtest 30 Aug](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1W_czkF9PVfYn2Zr7Vw_deF2xHfdQcV_Vt9Nm0gLu4Ho/edit?tab=t.0#heading=h.1yxgaotpt9bp).

## Session Recount

Five inhabitants of Solemtown, strangers until a few hours ago, walk through the streets, drunk. They are

 - **Zeke**, a demonologist who heard whispers in his head guiding him to the bed of the river Oria. There he found a tome of demon magic, from which he draws his magic powers.
 - **Rolf**, a saint-blooded boy come to Solemnity from afar. He is aware of his saint connection, but doesn't consider himself a noble.
 - **Michael**, a mutant that once had the misfortune to be too close to a spell gone wrong. Like a wet sponge over paint, Michaels facial features have been smeared and skewed.
 - **Phineas Alm (Phin)**, a farmhand hedge-mage who taught himself spells through observing the wandering magics through Solemtown.
 - **Ulrius of Death**, an elementalist specialising in the fowlest sort of magic.

They earn the ire of a couple rogues who are yelling nonsense at them. "Stop talking about us! We know you're talking about us! Shut up!"

The situation quickly devolves into a fight but the rogues are driven off. A few days later, **Michael** is carrying a message from the Church of Baronness Felicity to a boarding house in Longhouse Square. The message, it turns out, is a trap. Waiting for him there are 6 members of Ruxis Palatine, a gang based out of Mineria Ward, and their leader. Michael is threatened, and the ruxis leader demands 500 crowns for the insult they suffered in the street.

The group is trying to figure out what to do (**Michael** suggests going to the guards but **Phin** has a questionably acquired hand-cannon, so he doesn't want to) when they are interrupted by **Lord Fentworth-Pyxis-Anaein**, nephew to the leader of one of the wardens of Solemnity, commander of the gate watch, and lord of Fort Gale that protects the entrance to Solemtown. He has heard of their plight, and has an offer.

**Lord Fentworth** tells of **Lord Arnery-Talon**, husband to **Lady Selwa-Talon**, the warden of Solemtown and scion of House Talon, rival to House Pyxis-Anaein. **Lord Arnery** is known to collect artifacts from far off regions of the world. It is rumored these artifacts may be cursed. One such artifact was waylaid a few days ago by Ruxis Palatine. **Lord Fentworth** knows that the artifact is being kept at an abandoned farmstead on the hill overlooking Solemtown, and wants the characters to go get it. If they do this, and deliver whatever they found, he will pay them the debt they owe Ruxis Palatine and then some, as well as promising the favour of House Pyxis-Anaein.

The characters agree, having no real other options, and leave to persue the farmstead. Before they can get too far, they are accosted by the ostentacious **Qann Folecan**, a merchant in the employ of House Talon. He offers double whatever Fentworth offered them if they instead return the artifast to House Talon (how he knew what Fentworth told the party is something he does not disclose).

The party have no real choice but to retrieve the artifact, but they don't know what it even is, and now they must decide who to give to to. House Pyxis-Anaein or House Talon. Or perhaps the artifact is cursed - maybe even powerful - and they should keep it for themslves.

## Playtest notes

- Generating spells was probably the most fun thing to do at character creation. This is kind of a problem because only a subset of the players gets to do it. However, I don't think I'll change it, because the idea is the wizards get to have fun at character creation, but hit hardships in actual play that other characters don't. Oddly, it fits the "power now, consequences later" philosophy I have towards magic.

 - The vocations did a good job of getting players to figure out their backstories. My players are just good at that already, and they know this setting pretty well cos we played in it for ages, but I do think it gave them enough tangible details to form interesting stories around.

 - The players took to the spellcasting system like ducks to water, using their weird spell names in creative ways. Zeke's player used his 'Ooze's Incongruent Foot' spell to trip a rogue up.

 - It's become apparent that the spellcasting system isn't going to work out. Since it's semi-freeform, there are dials on what the spell can do mechanically, but there are only about 6 categories of mechanics and anything outside of those categories kind of doesn't work any more. I need some sort of 'spell complexity' dial that I can use to reign in the more outlandish ideas of the players.

 - Two enemies got down to 0 hit points today. One of them was from a death-element spell which aged him up, that was fun. The other was from a rapier which rolled the 'pierce' injury. Booorinnnng as fuckkk and definitely doesn't create the "oh shit something is about to go down" moment that I want from the injury system.

 - Combat still feels too turn based. May be how I handled running it, might be fixed with time.

 - I need some way of handling "If this happens, I want to do this" in combat. Since I don't want players thinking in turns, the rules really easily needs to accomodate this sort of contingency-based decision making.
