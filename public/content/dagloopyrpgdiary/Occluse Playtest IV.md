# Occluse Playtest IV

## Session Report

Two thieves burst into the room where the party rest. Without hestiation, **Michael** blasts with his crossbow, scoring an injury! He punctures the thief right through the eye. The thief drops his gun, which goes off, but the bullet misses.

The fight is stopped in it's tracks. The second thief, **Needle**, tries to help his friend. The party (who now realise **Kaleb** and **Rolf** have run off!) question the thieves. They learn that they've been here two days, unable to get past the undead in the room. A few days ago, they heard a loud noise ("like a dragons roar!") and everything went crazy. Their leader, **Arrowhead**, was killed and the pair locked themselves in the storeroom. They blame the wizard, **Orso**, who is the one that told the thieves to get the shiny. They don't know what it is, however.

The party raid the storeroom and wizards makeshift study. **Zeke** takes a box of magical reagents, including a box of pixie dust, a potion of read abyssal and a sealed jar of sainted blood. Quickly surmising the potions purpose, the party return to the table and the handless corpses. The thieves, **Needle** and **Garlic**, accompany them, seeking their dead leaders treasure.

**Zeke** quaffs the potion, and suddenly he can read the table. It says "They bore the true mark, and so I left them whole." **Zeke** quickly realised that, though he can now read the demon language, he cannot understand his native tongue, and so none of his companions. He does manage to use his spell _Oozes Telepathic Message and Orb_ to beam the meaning directly into **Phineas Alm**'s head. **Phin** gives the corpses a once over, and finds that the bodies who still have their hands have been branded with the 8 pointed star of chaos.

**Michael** commands **Needle** to pick the lock on the door. He does so, and the party descend a set of stairs. At the bottom, there is body, evidently dead but with no signs of injury. When **Phineas Alm** flips the body to steal his breastplate, he does spy some blood. When the breastplate is removed, they find that the mans clothing is untouched, but that his torso has a hole punched right through it. The wound is covered in soot.

Following directions to the captains chambers, the party come across a room absolutely splattered in gore. In the center of this room a black iron box sits inside a ring of 9 red candles. The candles have burned all the way down to the wax. The box is carved of solid metal, depciting a horned demons head on each face. The thieves recognise this as the object that was stolen. On the floor around the box, 9 silver locks lie broken open on the ground.

The party debate about how to handle this for a little while, then **Michael** touches the box. Ash pours from beneath the seam of the lid, and coalesces into a gigantic monstrous ashen beast. It's hulking body and arms erupt in a series of attacks.

**Phinease Alm** wastes no time. He whips out his hand cannon and fires a critical blast! The head of the shadow beast is blown straight off! But the beast is not dead, merely disoriented. This gives **Zeke** time to pelt it with spells, but also gives **Garlic** and **Needle** time to sneak off toward the treasure room. **Michael** tries to get them back, but has to save **Phineas Alm** with his spear. The party batter the ashen monster back for a while, destroying it's right arm. The monster is able to control the characters momentarily when it lands powerful blows.

The fight is going well until the monster bashes **Zeke** and steals his demonic book. Whatever this thing is, it can seemingly read the demon runes, and summons four men made of shadow. The shadow men are weak, but now that the party are outnumbered things start to go south. **Phineas Alm** is the first of the party to die. He is slaughtered by shadow men that tear the flesh off his bones. While **Garlic** and **Needle** get into a scuffle over the treasure in the back room, **Michael** retreats into the corridor. **Zeke** is grappled by the shadow men. Before he manages to use sorcery to turn himself into a cloud, the ashen monster shatters his femur against the bloody stone. Even when he transforms, he finds himself unable to escape the claws of the shadow men. In a last ditch effort, he tries to channel an oozing orb so strong it obliterates the ashen monster. His spell triggers chaos, but fortune, it seems, favours him. Both **Zeke** and the ashen monster dissapear, leaving behind nothing but an orb of acid that melts through the floor.

The ash men all disperse, leaving **Garlic** and **Michael** alone amongst the dead. **Michael** opens the box. It is lined with velvet cushioning. Laying on a red pillow within is a gnarled and twisted dead grey hand. A feeling compels the onlookers to grasp it, and **Michael** has the fastest impulse. As he holds the hand, it calls to him to fuse. It promises him great power. **Michael** acquiesces and allows the hand to take the place of his own. With an impulse drawn on from without, he grabs the face of **Garlic** and subjugates his mind. This is the first act of **Michael**, new scion of Baron Brandt, The Dominator. With the left hand of the greatest saint of chaos, **Michael** raises the corpses in the complex and leaves with 20 or so zombies under his control.

Outside he is met by **Orso**, who has been waiting for him to emerge. The hand tells **Michael** of a tomb in which the flower knights wait for him to control. To finish the work. To find more pieces of the dominator. To attain power unimaginable.

## Playtest Notes

 - At the start of the session when Michael shot Garlic's eye, he actually missed, but the damage roll scored its max so I allowed the shot to go wide. This "oh I failed, wait, no I didn't!" roller coaster is fun! A small chance of actually succeeding anyway keeps core to an idea that I really like, that being there is a tiny chance of things always working out.

 - The fight with the ash monster went a lot better than the other fights so far this game. It still took most of the session but it was interesting, and it was a boss fight so they're allowed to take a while.

 - New weapon actions worked kind of well for players, but I didn't use any standard effects against the players. I *did* use the lawful damage effects against the players though and it was a lot of fun! Very dynamic.

 - I think these rules are a bust. But, I've learned the value of playing! The way the rules get used at the table is really different than what I imagined, and I've chanelled everything I've learned into a new rules document that I will aim to playtest.

 - The big monster rules worked well! I just told the players which parts they could target and they had no problem with that. I don't know if it made the monster feel *bigger* at all, but no part of its scale really mattered anyway.

 - We got about 5 seconds to use the retainer rules. They were satisfactory I thought, nothing too interesting. The system of rolling morale to command them worked well, though I probably applied it too liberally. It also kind of stopped mattering when the fight broke out, then the retainers were in this weird space of kind of controlled kind of not. For dramas sake they kind of just broke out of being retainers, but it was okay the players bought it I think.

 - The biggest takeaway is that my design goal has really shifted to be about narrative action scenes in OSR gaming, more so than a rigorous injuries system. I want fights to be dangerous, but not necessarily deadly. The presence of a weapon should be nervewracking. The current design _sort of_ gets close to this, but gets lost in the weeds about injuries and daamge dice and fuzzy rules about mixed successes. Next game needs a clearer stance on when mixed successes come into play.
