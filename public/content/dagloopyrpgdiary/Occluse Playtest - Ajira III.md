# Occluse Playtest - Ajira III

## Session Report

Beyond the valley of the wildflowers, the path inclines sharply. The characters clamber upa mountain pass and find a fork in the road. Heading right, they find a cliff they must climb to progress. Rather than risk their lives, they backtrack and take the left path.

The path leads to a griffons nest! Two juicy eggs are guarded by a strangely docile griffon. **Tanawe** recognises that the wing of this griffon is broken. **Ryuku** tries to ingratiate himself with the noble beast and almost takes a horrid scratch to his arm, but it is blocked by **Kadian**. The beast is quickly put down while **Ŧaki** watches the sky for the griffons mate. The eggs are taken as rations.

With no other option, **Keppi** braves the climb up the cliff. Strong wind buffets him. Atop the cliff he finds a dilapidated hut. He's also beset by dizzy spells. **Ryuku** attempts the climb next, but begins to slip. Attempts at rescue by **Kadian** go awry and soon the two are clinging to the mountain for dear life. At this moment, **Keppi** is alerted to the presence of a man on the summit with him. This man turns out to be **Ahn**, a priest that watches the mountain. He quickly gets a rope and helps the rest of the party up the mountain.

The characters indulge this strange priests request to join him for dinner and have a night of raucous drinking and feasting. They are all beset by these dizzy spells. **Tanawe** tries to pry the old timers religion out of him but is met with hositility. Something is up with this priest - he claims to have a loving wife waiting at home but cannot remember her name. The superstitious party suspect some kind of god in disguise. The priest requests the party take his trinket off the mountain - a fingerbone in an ornate box. They accept. The next morning, the priest is gone. The 'glorious feast' they ate revealed to be moss and twigs. The sake they drank, mere water.

Continuing on through the pass, the characters must pass through The Valley of Shadow. The sun becomes a distant pale dot above them as they descend into this shadowy domain. Here they are approached by the Omukashi - contorted men with centipedal limbs. They just want a drop of blood each, but when **Ŧaki** acquiesces to their request, the only want more. Driven mad by its smell, the Omukashi attack. A brutal fight ensues, with **Ryuku** and **Keppi** taken grevious wounds. Still, the party emergy victorious. They continue their quest through the valley of shadow.


## Playtest Notes

 - I'm beginning to wonder what I want from my combat system again. I've been reading Traveller recently and thinking more about AD&D. I really like this idea of the combat encounter as a short, sharp resource expenditure as part of a wider exploration game loop. I wrote up [a quick set of rules for simulatenous combat](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1JgIwM0Z_nfpljRguWV-Ascmfzkf-xbUq1ee-jvLuYRU/edit?tab=t.g3986kdt60j#heading=h.g6uqphrrh89y) which I might try out next week.

 - Something else to consider is that my current taste might be moving in a direction not conducive to this game. Maybe I should start another thing instead of continuing to whittle this one into a different shape.

 - I have thoughts about injuries. They're kind of rare and don't do anything. I might transition injuries to be a matrix with damage on the left side and a d6 or 2d6 roll on the X axis, then have them proc of every (or most) hits. Will have to figure out how to retain some semblance of survivability for dungeon exploration, if that's something I need.

 - The scenario and make up of characters seems to really matter for playtests. In future I might make playtests a series of 2 session games with a specifically diversified set of character abilities so I get more coverage and don't try to micro-adjust the design to match the scenario.
