# Occluse Playtest III

## Session Report

**Rolf**, panicking, is making his way up the hill. He did not join his fellows at the farmstead, but now he thinks better of it. On his way he sees a man leading a cow into town at dusk. He pleads for help, and the newcomer, **Kaleb** agrees to go with him.

Initial investigation of the house goes awry when **Kaleb** spots blood. Fearing a trap, he attacks **Rolf**. In the cellar, **Phineas Alm** hears the noise. He breaks up the fight and threatens **Kaleb** with his hand cannon and strange demeanour. The trio return to the basement. **Ulrius of Death** (who provides no confidence to **Kaleb**) stays in the basement to keep watch.

Within the cramped stone tunnels of the underground complex, the party come into a hall with two doors. Ahead, **Kaleb** hears a woman. She is saying "**Siranon**, come here. Do not be afraid. Open the door, **Siranon**, I am here." Back in the line, **Rolf** opens a door to the side of the passage. It releases a trap! A board stuck full of nails punctures **Rolfs** shoulder and he begins to bleed prefusely all over his shirt. **Phineas Alm** bandages him up while the party, desperately in want of some room to maneuver, pile into the room beyond.

**Michael** ventures out from the safe point and opens the door they heard the woman through. Inside is a woman and four dead bodies. She is covered in blood and bears a patch with a serpentine S emblazoned on it. She beckons **Michael** with more soothing words, but they do not have their desired effect. She ignores his warning to stay back and catches a crossbow bolt in the stomach for it. She does not appear to notice as she bleeds out on to the ground, and dies. In her belt they discover a knife, and find that all the bodies in the room have been stabbed in the back.

Two doors lead away from the chamber. Smoke scent clouds the air. The party find a table with 6 corpses seated. Three are missing their left hands. Down another passage is a brazier inscribed with runes of blood. A gray powder rests in the bowl.

The way further ahead is locked, so they turn back to the trapped room and press on in that direction.

They find a room in which four thieves stand. Their backs are to the party, and they do not seem to notice their arrival. They do not seem to notice anything, in fact. One of the men seems not even to notice that he has lost a substantial amount of blood to a head wound. He stands motionless in a pile of blood. **Zan** is not one to stand idle, he leaps forward and attacks!

The men do respond to violence, and a bloody melee ensues. They do not respond to pain at all, even when struck down and killed. After the first man falls, he does not stay down! He rises again as his flesh sloughs off his body, leaving nothing but hard bone. **Zan** is cut down during the fighting, succumbing to his wounds. The party eventually fell the skeletons.

They do not have much of a respite, however. The door to the next chamber bursts open. Beyond it, a thief is crouched on one knee, a pistol levelled at the party. He yells, "Don't move!"

## Playtest Notes

I'm writing these a couple weeks after the fact so I kind of forgot.

 - The skeleton fight was too long and kind of boring. The battlefield still feels pretty static. I realised after grasping at my rules for something interesting that weapon effects should really make some meaningful actionable change.

 - The mana rules are a bit lacking. It's not that much easier to parse what a spell should do. In the next iteration I scrapped the mana mechanic and went to an attrition system based on accruing chaos. That's another thing - chaos. It hasn't been triggered once! And my casters have been blasting!

 - We used a new rule where fighters who miss attacks can choose to hit instead, but face a damage roll from the opponent. This worked kind of well, but the damage effects were so lame that it essentially means you have to roll an injury for anything interesting to happen. That does happen somewhat often though. Still, better weapon effects were written after this session.

 - Last time I said:

> I'm thinking up some more dynamic combat rules. Something along the line of "if you haven't used your action yet, you can declare your action in response to an enemy". 

I didn't end up using this at all.
